**Decisions the brief did not settle**
1. The binding is a request setting plus one definer; the agent's `own` read branch no longer matches rows it owns (an agent never owns a row, and the matrix had made it the owner). Write policies are untouched, so the bound agent writes nothing: `messaging.send:own` lets it propose a reply, never queue one.
2. A bound run reads what the customer and lead policies chain to, which includes the lead's quotes, orders, sizings, calls and timeline (customer-specific prices among them). The Concierge's fact reader (step 5) will read only the brief's list; if the lead wants RLS itself to keep quote and order values from an agent, that is a further clause on those tables.
3. The reply's subject is the customer (`account`), so a thread whose customer has no open lead can still get a reply; qualification and callback need the thread's lead.
4. The draft is marked through a new `actionIdKey` on the action type, filled by the approval; `messaging.reply.send` is a command of its own that reuses `messaging.message.send`'s queueing, rather than an option on W1's command.
5. Fixed forms are structural, not fixed wording (the words are the client's, §13 question 11): the filter checks the form's shape (question or not, beside a callback or a handoff) and holds every figure to what the run read.
6. Brackets of every kind, any `@` address (email or UPI, even at the company's own domain) and IFSC codes are refused; dates are compared by day and month; amounts by value with lakh and crore.
7. Promise words are a new named default for the sales head (seventeen, English and Roman Hindi); `callbackDaysAhead` (14) is a new named default in `AGENT_DEFAULTS.concierge`.
